"Words per minute" sounds obvious until you ask what a word is. *I* and *consequently* are both words. Typing tests solve this with a convention, and once you know it you can work out any typing score by hand.

## What counts as a word in WPM?

Five characters. Every keystroke counts, including spaces and punctuation, and the total is divided by five. So the sentence *The quick brown fox* is 19 characters, or 3.8 standard words, even though it has four dictionary words.

The formula:

**Gross WPM = (characters typed ÷ 5) ÷ minutes**

Example: 1,250 characters in 5 minutes is 1,250 ÷ 5 = 250 words, ÷ 5 minutes = **50 WPM**.

## What is the difference between gross and net WPM?

Gross WPM counts everything you typed. Net WPM takes off the errors you left uncorrected:

**Net WPM = gross WPM − (uncorrected errors ÷ minutes)**

In the example above, 10 uncorrected errors over 5 minutes cost 2 WPM, so the net speed is **48 WPM**. Each error is treated as costing a whole word, which is deliberate: a document with a typo in it is not finished.

Different tests apply the penalty differently, and some count only correctly typed words. That is why the same person scores differently on different sites. Compare your scores on one test, not across tests.

## How is typing accuracy calculated?

**Accuracy = correct characters ÷ total characters typed × 100**

If you typed 1,250 characters and 25 were wrong, your accuracy is 1,225 ÷ 1,250 = **98%**. Keystrokes you corrected still count as errors in most accuracy figures, because the backspace cost you time.

## How do WPM, CPM and KPH convert?

They are the same measurement in different units.

| Unit | Meaning | Conversion |
|---|---|---|
| WPM | Words per minute | characters per minute ÷ 5 |
| CPM | Characters per minute | WPM × 5 |
| KPH | Keystrokes per hour | WPM × 300 |

So 40 WPM is 200 CPM and 12,000 KPH. A data-entry job advert asking for 10,000 KPH is asking for about 33 WPM. Numeric data entry is often quoted in KPH because the keystrokes are digits, not words.

## What is a good typing speed?

The best public evidence is a 2018 study from Aalto University and the University of Cambridge, which collected 136 million keystrokes from 168,000 volunteers:

- The **average was about 52 WPM**.
- The fastest group typed well over 100 WPM.
- Faster typists made **fewer** errors, not more.
- Faster typists often pressed the next key before releasing the previous one.

The volunteers chose to take an online typing test, so they probably type more than the general population; treat 52 as a generous average. As a rough guide for an adult who types daily: 40 WPM is functional, 60 is comfortable, and above 80 is fast. What a job needs depends on the job, so ask the employer which test and which figure they use.

## Does a one-minute test give a fair number?

It gives a high one. Short tests reward a sprint and cover few of the awkward letter combinations. A three- or five-minute test on unfamiliar prose is closer to real work. Common words and lower-case text also score higher than text with capitals, numbers and punctuation, so check what a test contains before comparing two scores.

## How do you raise your WPM?

Mostly by removing errors and hesitations rather than by moving your fingers faster.

1. **Fix the mapping.** Use the same finger for the same key every time.
2. **Stop looking down.** Each glance at the keyboard is a pause.
3. **Practise at the edge of accuracy.** Slow down until you are near error-free, then let the pace creep up.
4. **Drill your slow keys.** Everyone has a few: often the bottom row, numbers and the little-finger keys.
5. **Practise a little every day.**

The method is covered step by step in [how to learn touch typing](/typing-mill/blog/how-to-learn-touch-typing/).

## Frequently asked questions

### How many characters is one word in a typing test?

Five, counting spaces and punctuation. Total characters divided by five gives the number of standard words.

### Is 50 WPM good?

It is close to the average of about 52 WPM found among 168,000 online volunteers, and enough for most office work.

### What is 10,000 KPH in WPM?

About 33 WPM. Divide keystrokes per hour by 300.

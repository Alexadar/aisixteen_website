How can you tell whether the aurora will be visible tonight?

By AISixteenPublished November 24, 20265 min read

Short answerCheck three things in order: the forecast Kp and view line for tonight, then, on the night, whether the solar wind's Bz is pointing south and staying there, then your own sky: darkness, cloud and a clear view toward the pole. Solar wind readings give roughly 30 to 60 minutes of warning.

An aurora forecast is really several forecasts with different ranges. Some look days ahead and are rough; one looks less than an hour ahead and is quite good. Knowing which is which tells you when to go to bed and when to put your boots on.

What actually causes an aurora?

Charged particles from the Sun flow past Earth as the solar wind, carrying a magnetic field with them. When that field points south, opposite to Earth's field on the day side, the two connect and energy is loaded into Earth's magnetic tail. When it is released, electrons run down the field lines and hit the upper atmosphere at about 100 to 300 km, where oxygen and nitrogen glow: green and red from oxygen, blue and purple from nitrogen.

The light forms a ring around each magnetic pole, the auroral oval. More energy makes the oval brighter and pushes it toward the equator. Every forecast is an estimate of where that oval will be.

How far ahead can each forecast see?

What you check Looks ahead How reliable What it tells you
Coronal hole or eruption seen on the Sun 1 to 4 days Rough: arrival times can be off by half a day, and the field direction is unknown Whether a night is worth keeping free
Kp forecast and tonight's view line Hours to 3 days Moderate How far from the pole the aurora might reach
Solar wind at L1: speed, density, Bz About 30 to 60 minutes Good Whether it is about to start
The sky itself Now Certain Whether you can see it

Why is Bz the number to watch?

Bz is the north–south part of the magnetic field in the solar wind. It is the switch.

  • Bz south (negative): the door is open. Around −5 nT sustained is enough for activity at high latitudes; −10 nT or lower, held for an hour or more, is storm material.
  • Bz north (positive): the door is mostly closed, even if the wind is fast.

Speed and density set how much arrives while the door is open. A quiet wind is 300 to 500 km/s; above 600 km/s with southward Bz is a strong combination.

Bz can flip in minutes, which is why a promising evening can go quiet and a dull one can light up. Watch the trend over the last half hour, not a single reading.

How much warning do solar wind readings give?

The solar wind is measured by spacecraft at the L1 point, about 1.5 million km from Earth toward the Sun. The warning is the time the wind takes to cover that distance:

Solar wind speed Travel time from L1
400 km/s about 62 minutes
500 km/s about 50 minutes
600 km/s about 42 minutes
800 km/s about 31 minutes

So the faster and more promising the wind, the less warning you get. NOAA's short-term aurora model, OVATION, takes those L1 measurements and produces a map of the oval for roughly the next 30 to 90 minutes.

What is the view line?

The view line is the edge of where an aurora might be seen, which is farther from the pole than where it is overhead. Because the light is 100 km or more up, an aurora overhead at one place can be seen low on the horizon from several hundred kilometres toward the equator. NOAA publishes a view line for tonight and tomorrow night. If you are near the line, expect a glow or pillars low on the poleward horizon rather than curtains overhead.

Does a high Kp guarantee an aurora where I am?

No. Kp is a planet-wide, three-hour average. It tells you how far the oval reached, not what was above you at 23:10. You can miss a display at Kp 7 under cloud, and people at high latitudes see aurora most clear nights at Kp 2. Use Kp to answer "is it my latitude's turn?" and Bz to answer "is it happening now?".

What on the ground still decides it?

  • Darkness. The sky has to be properly dark. At high latitudes in summer it never is, which is why the aurora season runs from late August to April.
  • Cloud. Aurora is far above all weather. Solid cloud ends the night.
  • Light pollution and the Moon. A faint aurora is easily lost in city glow or under a full Moon. Get a dark horizon toward the pole.
  • Time of night. Activity is most likely in the hours around local midnight, though strong storms ignore the clock.
  • Time of year. Geomagnetic activity is statistically higher in the weeks around the March and September equinoxes.
  • Your eyes. Give them 15 to 20 minutes in the dark. A faint aurora looks grey-white to the eye; a phone camera on a long exposure will show the green first.

A simple routine for the night

  1. Afternoon: look at the forecast Kp and tonight's view line. If the line is far poleward of you, relax.
  2. After dark: check cloud, then check Bz and speed. Southward and steady, go out.
  3. Outside: look toward the pole, low on the horizon, and wait. Displays come in bursts of 10 to 30 minutes with quiet gaps between.

Frequently asked questions

What Bz is good for aurora?

Negative, meaning southward. Around −5 nT sustained helps at high latitudes; −10 nT or lower for an hour or more usually means a storm.

How long before an aurora do you get a warning?

About 30 to 60 minutes from solar wind measurements at L1, less when the wind is fast. Forecasts days ahead exist but are rough.

What Kp do I need to see the aurora?

It depends on your magnetic latitude. Near the Arctic Circle, Kp 1 to 3 is often enough; the northern United States and northern Europe typically need Kp 5 to 7; lower latitudes need a severe storm.
